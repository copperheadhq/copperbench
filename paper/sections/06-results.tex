\subsection{Consistency across repeats}

Pass@1 reports the expected outcome of a single user run; pass\textsuperscript{k} reports the fraction of tasks that passed every repeat. The gap between them is the reliability story.

\subsection{Refusal correctness}

Correct-refusal rate is measured over tasks whose expected outcome is a refusal; false-refusal rate is measured over tasks with a correct edit. A model cannot improve one without exposure on the other, which is the point of mixing the classes.
